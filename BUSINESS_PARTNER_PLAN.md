# GGuard Diagnostics — Business Partner Plan

Prepared for prospective operating partners, mentors, pilot customers, and strategic advisors
Stage: pre-revenue, pre-pilot
Base market: Greater Phoenix, Arizona
Planning horizon: 36 months

## 1. Executive summary

GGuard Diagnostics is building an independent, evidence-based assessment service for garage-door repairs. A homeowner, HOA, or property manager submits photographs, video, symptoms, and an optional contractor quote. Software prepares a structured diagnostic draft and price analysis; a qualified human expert reviews the evidence before a report is released. Each completed report can produce a tamper-evident maintenance record for the property.

The customer problem is not simply “finding a repair contractor.” It is the information imbalance that occurs after a contractor recommends a repair. Most owners cannot independently determine whether the diagnosis, scope, urgency, or price is reasonable. Property managers and HOA boards face the same problem across many properties and must also document why a repair was approved.

GGuard’s initial wedge is garage doors because the category is visual, safety-sensitive, frequently quoted in the home, and narrow enough to build repeatable assessment protocols. The long-term opportunity is a neutral verification layer for home-maintenance decisions across additional trades.

GGuard has a working product foundation but no revenue or pilot evidence yet. The immediate objective is not a national launch or fundraising round. It is a 90-day validation program that produces five design partners, 50–100 completed assessment cases, measurable customer outcomes, and a repeatable sales motion.

The company is seeking an operating business partner with property-management or home-services distribution experience. The ideal person will own customer discovery, pilots, partnerships, positioning, and revenue while helping shape a trustworthy operational model. A generic social-media marketer is not the right partner.

## 2. Company purpose

### Mission

Give property owners an independent, understandable second opinion before they authorize a consequential home repair.

### Vision

Become the trusted verification and maintenance-history layer between property owners, managers, contractors, insurers, and future buyers.

### Brand promise

Neutral. Evidence-based. Human-verified. Actionable.

### What GGuard is not

- It is not a contractor lead-generation marketplace.
- It does not perform repairs or accept referral payments that influence recommendations.
- It does not promise that AI alone can diagnose safety-critical equipment.
- It is not yet a blockchain company; tamper-evident records are a supporting feature, not the customer’s primary reason to buy.
- It is not a replacement for an in-person inspection when the evidence is insufficient or a safety issue requires escalation.

## 3. Customer problem

### Homeowners

- Do not know whether a proposed repair is necessary.
- Cannot distinguish a safety issue from an upsell.
- Lack a reliable price range for the exact scope being quoted.
- Often make a decision while the door is stuck and time pressure is high.
- Have no durable, standardized record of the diagnosis and evidence.

### Property managers

- Review maintenance decisions across dozens or hundreds of homes.
- Need faster approval without blindly trusting every vendor quote.
- Need documentation for owners and residents.
- Have difficulty comparing contractor performance and pricing across a portfolio.
- Lose maintenance context when staff, owners, tenants, or vendors change.

### HOAs

- Need consistent handling across units.
- Must explain expenditures to boards and homeowners.
- Need evidence for compliance, reserve planning, and vendor oversight.
- Want to avoid the appearance of favoritism toward a contractor.

### Contractors

Ethical contractors also benefit. A neutral record can validate legitimate safety recommendations, document completed work, and separate professional operators from low-quality competitors. Contractor participation must remain structurally separate from GGuard’s assessment decision.

## 4. Solution and customer journey

### Homeowner journey

1. Customer describes symptoms and submits at least two guided photos, optional video, and a quote.
2. Customer pays for the selected service level.
3. Software organizes evidence and drafts findings, limitations, price ranges, and questions.
4. A qualified reviewer examines the actual evidence and edits or rejects the draft.
5. Customer receives a plain-language report with recommendation, safety warnings, fair-price range, and questions for the contractor.
6. If work is later completed, a repair record can be linked to the property history.

### Property-manager and HOA journey

1. Organization creates a workspace and receives resident/tenant submission links.
2. Residents submit evidence against the organization’s account.
3. The organization sees status, quotes, findings, and portfolio trends.
4. GGuard credits or per-report charges cover completed assessments.
5. The organization exports a decision record for the owner, board, or property file.

### Service boundaries

Every report must clearly state:

- what evidence was reviewed;
- what could and could not be determined remotely;
- whether the customer should stop operating the door;
- whether an in-person inspection is required;
- that prices vary by door, region, parts, urgency, and contractor;
- that GGuard does not perform the repair.

## 5. Initial market strategy

GGuard should not claim a large top-down market number before pilot data exists. The credible market case is bottom-up.

### Beachhead

Greater Phoenix residential property managers and HOA/community-association managers with 100–2,000 units under management.

Why Phoenix:

- The founder can build local relationships and observe pilot operations directly.
- Phoenix has active local channels for both property managers and community associations.
- The Phoenix Metro NARPM chapter holds recurring meetings where affiliates can address members.
- CAI Arizona reports nearly 900 members, including more than 350 community managers and over 200 businesses.

### Bottom-up validation model

These are planning assumptions, not claimed market facts:

| Input | Conservative validation assumption |
|---|---:|
| Local design partners in first 90 days | 5 |
| Average units represented per partner | 250 |
| Garage-door cases per 100 units per year | 8–15 |
| Assessments generated by initial partners/year | 100–188 |
| Blended B2B revenue per completed case | $22–$35 |
| Annual platform/account fee per partner | $1,800–$6,000 |

The first pilots must establish the real incident rate, submission completion rate, turnaround time, price sensitivity, and savings/documentation value. Those numbers—not an internet market-size statistic—should drive the next forecast.

### Expansion sequence

1. Phoenix property managers.
2. Phoenix-area HOA/community managers.
3. Arizona statewide through trade associations and referrals.
4. Adjacent Sun Belt markets with similar single-family housing concentration.
5. Additional visually assessable trades only after the garage-door workflow has strong accuracy and unit economics.

## 6. Competitive positioning

### Alternatives customers use today

- Accept the first contractor’s recommendation.
- Call a second contractor and repeat an in-person sales visit.
- Search generic repair-price articles.
- Ask a property manager or maintenance coordinator for an opinion.
- Use a home warranty or insurer’s vendor network.
- Upload photos to a general-purpose AI assistant without expert accountability.

### GGuard differentiation

| Dimension | GGuard position |
|---|---|
| Incentive | Does not earn more when a larger repair is sold |
| Evidence | Uses customer-submitted media and quote details |
| Review | Human reviewer is accountable for final release |
| Output | Structured decision report, not an informal opinion |
| Portfolio | Organization-level records and quote comparisons |
| History | Tamper-evident report and repair records |
| Speed | Remote intake without scheduling a second truck roll |

### Defensibility over time

The website itself is not the moat. Defensibility must come from:

- a proprietary labeled evidence set tied to human-reviewed outcomes;
- trade-specific assessment protocols and safety rules;
- reviewer quality assurance and calibration data;
- integrations into property-management workflows;
- portfolio pricing benchmarks normalized by geography and job scope;
- trusted neutrality and documented conflict-of-interest rules;
- accumulated property maintenance records and repeat usage.

## 7. Business model

### Direct-to-consumer pricing currently contemplated

- Standard: $39, 24-hour target.
- Express: $79, priority target.
- Comprehensive: $99, expanded analysis.

These should be treated as price tests. Do not promise turnaround until reviewer capacity has been proven.

### Recommended B2B pilot offer

For the first five organizations:

- 60-day design-partner pilot.
- No platform fee.
- First 10 completed reports included.
- $20–$30 per completed report thereafter.
- Weekly 20-minute feedback call.
- Permission to publish an anonymized case study only after written approval.
- No annual contract during validation.

### Post-pilot B2B model to test

- Platform fee based on units under management.
- Included monthly assessment credits.
- Usage fee for additional completed reports.
- Enterprise pricing for integrations, custom reporting, service levels, and multi-office portfolios.

Avoid locking in the website’s present HOA annual prices before testing them; they are likely too low to support human review and customer success.

### Future revenue options

- Portfolio analytics subscription.
- API/integration fees for property-management platforms.
- White-labeled verification for insurers or home-warranty administrators.
- Paid in-person escalation through independent inspectors, with transparent separation from repair sales.
- Additional trade modules.

GGuard should not sell contractor leads or accept success fees based on repair value. That would undermine the core neutrality claim.

## 8. Go-to-market plan

### Phase 1: founder-led discovery, weeks 1–4

Target 30 interviews:

- 12 property managers or maintenance coordinators.
- 8 HOA/community managers or board members.
- 5 garage-door experts, including retired or non-selling professionals.
- 3 insurance/home-warranty claims professionals.
- 2 homeowners who recently purchased repairs.

Interview goals:

- Understand the current approval workflow.
- Quantify delays, second visits, disputes, and over-budget cases.
- Learn who owns the budget and who feels the pain.
- Test whether “independent second opinion,” “quote verification,” or “maintenance decision record” is the strongest message.
- Obtain real, anonymized historical cases for workflow testing.

Do not pitch for the first 15 minutes. Learn the workflow first.

### Phase 2: design-partner pilots, weeks 5–12

- Enroll five organizations.
- Train one contact at each partner.
- Process 50–100 total cases.
- Hold weekly pilot reviews.
- Record time-to-report, evidence sufficiency, reviewer time, outcome, customer action, and estimated savings or avoided truck roll.
- Produce two approved case studies.

### Phase 3: repeatable local sales, months 4–9

- Convert at least three pilots to paid accounts.
- Establish one local property-management integration or documented workflow.
- Join or attend Phoenix Metro NARPM and CAI Arizona as an appropriate affiliate/business partner.
- Run an educational session: “How to document and independently validate residential maintenance quotes.”
- Build a referral program for property managers, not a paid contractor lead program.

### Messaging hierarchy

Primary: “Get an independent, human-reviewed second opinion before approving a garage-door repair.”

For PMs: “Approve repairs faster, document the decision, and compare quotes across your portfolio.”

For HOAs: “Create consistent, defensible maintenance decisions across the community.”

Avoid leading with AI or blockchain. Those are implementation details. Trust and decision quality are the product.

## 9. Pilot scorecard

The pilot is successful only if it produces measurable evidence.

| Metric | 90-day target |
|---|---:|
| Customer-discovery interviews | 30 |
| Signed design partners | 5 |
| Submitted cases | 50–100 |
| Cases with sufficient evidence | ≥70% |
| Median standard turnaround | <24 hours |
| Human reviewer time/case | <20 minutes |
| Partner users who submit a second case | ≥50% |
| Reports rated useful | ≥80% |
| Pilot-to-paid conversions | ≥3 |
| Serious safety misses | 0 |

Additional learning metrics:

- percentage agreeing with the original contractor;
- percentage requiring more evidence or in-person escalation;
- average difference between quoted price and reviewed fair range;
- approval-cycle time before and after GGuard;
- evidence upload abandonment rate;
- acquisition source and cost;
- gross margin per assessment.

## 10. Operations and quality

### Reviewer model

Start with a small contracted reviewer panel rather than an open marketplace.

Requirements:

- documented garage-door service experience;
- conflict-of-interest disclosure;
- no selling repairs to the reviewed customer;
- standardized rubric and safety escalation protocol;
- periodic blind calibration cases;
- professional liability review with counsel and insurer.

### Quality controls

- Minimum evidence checklist before payment or review.
- AI output never released without human approval.
- High-risk findings require a second review or automatic in-person referral.
- Every report records evidence, reviewer, model/version, limitations, and timestamps.
- Customer correction and dispute workflow.
- Monthly sample audit of completed reports.
- Clear retention and deletion schedule for photos and PII.

### Legal and compliance priorities

Before commercial launch, obtain qualified legal advice regarding:

- business entity and founder/partner agreements;
- professional liability and errors-and-omissions insurance;
- whether reports could be considered inspections or regulated advice in target states;
- privacy policy, terms, consent, biometric/media considerations, and data retention;
- AI disclosures and recordkeeping;
- contractor licensing language;
- marketing substantiation for savings and accuracy claims;
- dispute process and limitation of liability.

## 13. Funding strategy

### Recommended sequence

1. Keep spending lean through customer discovery.
2. Seek non-dilutive mentoring and accelerator support.
3. Fund the 90-day pilot through founder capital, small grants, or a limited friends-and-family SAFE only if necessary.
4. Consider an angel/pre-seed raise after three paid organizations, repeat usage, reliable report delivery, and evidence of gross margin.

### Illustrative use of a $150,000 pre-seed round

- 35% product/security and infrastructure.
- 25% reviewer operations and quality.
- 20% founder-led sales and pilot success.
- 10% legal, insurance, privacy, and accounting.
- 10% contingency.

Do not raise primarily to buy advertising. Until retention and unit economics are known, paid marketing amplifies uncertainty.

## 15. Partner structure and protection

Do not give away half the company after several conversations.

Recommended process:

1. Two-week mutual diligence: goals, availability, finances, values, and references.
2. Four-to-six-week working trial with written deliverables: 15 interviews, five pilot proposals, one refined sales deck, and a jointly owned operating plan.
3. Review actual behavior: follow-through, honesty, customer skill, conflict handling, and pace.
4. Only then negotiate a cofounder role.

For a genuine cofounder, use:

- four-year vesting;
- one-year cliff;
- IP assignment and confidentiality;
- clear roles and decision rights;
- founder departure and repurchase provisions;
- written treatment of existing code and prior IP;
- board and deadlock provisions;
- attorney-reviewed founder agreement.

Equity depends on commitment, timing, replacement cost, risk, and whether both parties will work full time. It should not be decided from a generic percentage table. If the candidate remains part-time indefinitely, use milestone compensation, commission, options, or advisor equity instead of cofounder-level ownership.

## 19. Principal risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Remote evidence is insufficient | Guided capture, minimum evidence rules, refund/escalation path |
| Incorrect safety recommendation | Human review, critical-case protocol, second review, insurance |
| Customers do not pay for second opinions | B2B pilots, test pricing, quantify approval and documentation value |
| Contractors perceive GGuard as hostile | Publish neutral standards; validate correct quotes as well as challenge weak ones |
| AI creates liability or hallucinations | Never release raw AI output; log models and human changes |
| Human review destroys margins | Measure reviewer minutes; templates; narrow scope; tiered escalation |
| Partner channel is slow | Run PM and HOA discovery in parallel; stay local initially |
| “Blockchain” distracts buyers | Lead with decision quality; treat anchoring as optional proof infrastructure |
| New partner relationship fails | Working trial, vesting, role clarity, references, founder agreement |
| Product is marketed before it is secure | Complete launch checklist and controlled pilots before public promotion |

## 21. The specific ask

GGuard is seeking three different forms of help and should not confuse them:

1. **Mentor:** two meetings per month for six months, focused on validation, pricing, and partner selection. No equity expected.
2. **Design partner:** 60-day pilot, weekly feedback, real cases, and permission to measure outcomes. Receives favorable pilot terms, not founder equity.
3. **Operating partner/cofounder candidate:** owns discovery and revenue work during a four-to-six-week trial, then may negotiate a formal vesting role based on demonstrated commitment and fit.

The immediate presentation ask should be:

> Help GGuard secure five Phoenix-area property-management or HOA design partners and validate 50–100 real assessment cases over the next 90 days. If we work well together and the evidence supports the business, we will define a long-term operating partnership with vesting, clear ownership, and shared milestones.
